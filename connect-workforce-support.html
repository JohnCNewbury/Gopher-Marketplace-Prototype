Crews & headcount

How staffing more than one person works

This is where a marketplace behaves differently from a staffing agency, so it is worth being plain about it.

Two ways to staff it — and the choice is yours.

When a shift needs more than one person, you decide how the crew gets built: pick every worker yourself, or hire one contractor to assemble the whole crew for you. Both sit on the same post under Labor management, and the choice is Connect-only — it is the difference between a marketplace that makes you take one approach and one that lets you match the job in front of you.

  • Select each worker individually — the fastest route. You review everyone who accepts and approve them one at a time, so every person on the shift is someone you chose — which is why the flags on the cards above read 1 of 2 crew. You can start before every seat is filled. Bids are not available on this option.
  • Hire a contractor to assemble your crew — your post goes to contractors, and the one you hire is responsible for finding every worker the shift needs. One point of contact instead of a stack of approvals, and you can take bids on it.
  • Headcount — Say how many people the shift needs. The post stays open until the seats are filled.
  • Repeat crews — Save the ones who worked out to MY Gophers, and prioritize them the next time you post.
  • Hours — Set the window and the duration. Workers see both before they accept, so nobody arrives surprised.
Be realistic about last-minute fills. Coverage depends on how many Gophers are active near you at that hour, and that genuinely varies by market — Gopher iQ will tell you what your area looks like rather than promising a crew we cannot see. Single-seat, same-day shifts fill most reliably; a six-person overnight posted at 4 PM is a harder ask. Post early where you can, and open it to bids if you would rather compare than guess at the rate.

Describe the shift

Headcount, hours, location, what the work involves, and anything they need to bring or wear. A photo of the space helps.

Set what you'll pay

Your offer is what each worker earns — 100% of it. Or open it to bids and compare.

3

Pick who takes it

Review profiles, ratings, and past jobs. Hire, consider, or deny — and save the good ones to MY Gophers.

4

Confirm when it's done

Confirm and rate on completion, which releases payment. Something not right? Dispute instead and give them a chance to fix it.
