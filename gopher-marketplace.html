Four kinds of people. One Gopher in the middle. Neighbors get help, Service Providers get paid, businesses fill the gaps in their crew, and merchants bring the block through their door. Same account. Same neighborhood. Same day.

The whole idea in three lines

You name what you need and what you'll pay. A trusted local Gopher says yes. Gopher holds the money until you confirm the job's done.

Businesses hire the same way for shifts, crews, and Pros. Merchants post Deals for free and let a Gopher bring them to your door.

Gopher is always in the middle. That's why the ratings are real, the prices are up front, and nobody chases a payment.

The four

Four hats, not four kinds of people.

Each hat has its own app. Any neighbor can wear more than one.

Neighbors

People who need something done today.

A delivery, a move, a haul, a ride, a hand around the house. Describe it, name your price, and a local Gopher comes to you.

What they get: real ratings from people who actually paid, a price that's set before anyone shows up, and zero haggling.

Service Providers

The neighbors who do the work. We call them Gophers.

Set up once, pick the jobs you want, and get paid by Gopher the moment the customer confirms. No invoices to chase.

What they get: every interested job on one screen, the right to counter, and 100% of the posted price. The fee is the customer's, not theirs.

Businesses

Companies with a gap in the crew.

Couriers, cleaners, trades, event staff, warehouse hands. Describe the work, name the pay, and qualified local Gophers fill the shift.

What they get: the same Gophers neighbors trust, with Pro credentials on file and the paperwork a business needs.

Merchants

Local shops, restaurants, and eligible Service Providers.

Post a Deal for free. Everyone on Gopher sees it, and so does anyone on the web. Customers buy from you directly.

What they get: local reach with no fee to post, and a Gopher who'll deliver the order without taking a slice of it.
Same person
Merchant and Business aren't two kinds of company. They're two things one shop does.

A pizza place posts a Deal as a merchant, hires a driver for Friday night as a business, and the owner picks up a $50 delivery on the way home as a Gopher. One account. Three hats. Same day.

What a Gopher sees
Trust, both directions

Neighbors are verified for Gophers. Gophers are vetted for neighbors.

That's why a stranger from an app isn't a stranger.

Both people in this kitchen know exactly who the other one is. That's the whole point.

TrustShield™ · for neighbors

A neighbor earns the TrustShield when their identity is verified: name, photo, address, and contact info. It sits next to their name on every request, so a Gopher knows the person they're about to help is exactly who they say they are.

One badge. You have it or you don't.

Where Gophers see it
  • Next to your name on every request you post
  • On your profile in the Gopher Go app
  • On confirmation emails and receipts

The worker ladder · for Gophers

Every Gopher, including standard, has their identity verified through Stripe with a government ID and Social Security number. Badges stack on top of that.

Standard

Identity verified. Government ID and SSN on file.

Elite adds a clean criminal background check.

Elite+ is Elite plus a clean DMV record. If you're Elite+, you're also Elite.

Pro is separate: licensed, bonded, and/or insured. A Gopher can be Pro without being Elite, or Pro and Elite+ at once.

The merchant's edge

Deals: the free last mile.

Delivery apps take up to 30% of a merchant's order. Gopher Deals takes nothing to post, and a Gopher brings it to the customer for a price the customer already agreed to.

1
The merchant posts a Deal. Free.

No fee to post, no fee on the order. Any local shop, restaurant, or eligible Service Provider can offer one.

2
Everyone sees it.

Every neighbor, Gopher, and business on the marketplace, plus anyone browsing the web. You need an account to redeem it, not to see it.

3
The customer buys direct from the merchant.

Right in Gopher's web view. The merchant keeps the customer, the pricing, and the profit.

4
Tap for a Gopher to bring it.

Same flow, one more tap. A local Gopher picks it up and delivers it. That's the last mile, handled, without the merchant paying for it.

What a merchant keeps

30%

is what a merchant can hand to a delivery app on every order. On Gopher Deals the merchant pays nothing to post and nothing on the sale. The customer pays the Gopher's trip price plus Gopher's small fee. The merchant's margin is never touched.
